# Improve or rebuild? — website decision guide

## Purpose and status

A proposed free PDF/e-guide for business owners deciding whether their website needs focused improvements, a design refresh, or a rebuild. It should help them understand the decision and estimate what an investment would need to achieve to be worthwhile.

This is a content and UX outline, not a completed PDF or a working lead-capture flow. The homepage remains a design showcase. The existing free scan is also a preview; its sample finding is illustrative.

The guide supports Outfox Web's positioning around expert judgment. Useful recommendations may include keeping what works, making a few focused changes, or investigating the problem before committing to a larger project.

## Proposed PDF structure

Aim for six to eight concise pages, including worksheets. Each page should help the reader complete a task rather than fill space with general advice.

1. **Define the job.** Name the main action the website should help a visitor take, the business problem behind it, and one sign that things have improved.
2. **Locate the friction.** Separate an unclear message, a confusing visitor journey, dated presentation, technical problems, and limitations in managing the site. Mark what is observed and what is only suspected.
3. **Compare the routes.** Use the improve / refresh / rebuild matrix below. Readers may identify a likely route or discover that they need an expert review before choosing.
4. **Choose the first useful change.** Identify the highest-priority issue, why it matters, and the smallest intervention worth trying. A larger project should have a business reason.
5. **Estimate the value.** Record current enquiries, how many become customers, customer contribution, and the business's ability to serve additional demand. Include a route for readers without reliable data.
6. **Test the investment.** Compare cautious, expected, and stronger scenarios over a stated period. Calculate indicative payback and ROI using the worksheet below.
7. **Prepare for a conversation.** Summarize goals, evidence, priorities, constraints, and open questions. Explain what an expert needs to assess the situation and shape a proposal.
8. **Choose a next step.** Keep the current site, make focused improvements, request a review, or discuss a larger project. The conversation invitation should follow naturally from the worksheet.

## Decision matrix

This is orientation, not a diagnosis. A reader's answers suggest what to investigate; a site-specific review may change the recommendation.

| Route                | When it may fit                                                                                                             | What to investigate first                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Focused improvements | The site broadly supports the business, but a few specific problems get in the way.                                         | Which messages, pages, actions, or technical issues can be improved within the existing structure?             |
| Design refresh       | The underlying site works, but the presentation and hierarchy no longer communicate the offer well.                         | Can updated content and design resolve the problem without replacing the build?                                |
| Rebuild              | Important business needs have changed, or the site's structure and technical constraints prevent the required improvements. | Which constraints require replacement, what can be retained, and what value would the new capabilities create? |
| Expert review first  | The symptoms are visible, but the cause or appropriate scope is uncertain.                                                  | Which visitor journeys and business questions need a closer look before choosing a project?                    |

Include an explicit “keep what works” option. A useful guide should help a reader avoid unnecessary work as well as identify worthwhile investment.

## Value and ROI worksheet

Outfox Web prices projects around value and expected return. The worksheet prepares a discussion; it does not generate an Outfox quote or turn the service into a fixed package. Project timelines and the amount of guided collaboration are agreed for the particular engagement.

### Start with known information

Record the period each figure covers and where it came from:

- Current qualified website enquiries per month.
- The share of those enquiries that become customers.
- Average revenue from a customer and the additional cost of serving that customer.
- Current project-cost estimate, or a blank field to revisit after a proposal.
- Additional monthly website/operating costs compared with the current setup.
- Any measurable cash savings and the business's capacity to take on more work.

Use **contribution per additional customer**: revenue less the additional costs of serving that customer. Revenue by itself is not the financial benefit. If customer types vary substantially, use separate estimates or a documented mix.

If the reader lacks useful enquiry or sales data, the first recommendation is to establish a baseline. Do not substitute an industry conversion rate and present it as the reader's likely outcome.

### Estimate scenarios

Use a stated evaluation period, such as 12 months. The period is a worksheet choice, not a claim about project turnaround or guaranteed payback. Let the reader vary the assumptions; include a scenario with no enquiry growth.

For each scenario:

```text
Additional customers per month
  = additional qualified enquiries × enquiry-to-customer rate

Monthly net benefit
  = additional customers × contribution per customer
    + measurable monthly cash savings
    − additional monthly operating costs

Net return over the chosen period
  = total net benefit during that period − one-time investment

Estimated ROI (%), when investment is greater than zero
  = net return ÷ one-time investment × 100
```

If benefits are constant and positive, indicative payback in months is `one-time investment ÷ monthly net benefit`. If benefits vary or take time to start, track cumulative monthly benefit and find when it covers the investment instead. A non-positive benefit does not produce a finite payback in this model.

Count benefits only during the period when they could actually occur. Include implementation costs in the investment and account for launch timing. Time saved is useful capacity, but it is a cash saving only where expenditure is genuinely avoided. Avoid counting the same benefit as both extra customer contribution and time savings.

| Input or result                          | Cautious / no growth        | Expected                    | Stronger                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Additional qualified enquiries per month | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             |
| Enquiry-to-customer rate                 | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             |
| Contribution per customer                | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             |
| Additional costs and cash savings        | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             | Reader estimate             |
| Monthly net benefit                      | Calculated                  | Calculated                  | Calculated                  |
| Net return over the chosen period        | Calculated                  | Calculated                  | Calculated                  |
| Estimated ROI and payback                | Calculated where applicable | Calculated where applicable | Calculated where applicable |

Explain uncertainty in plain language: the website is one influence among traffic quality, the offer, sales follow-up, and service capacity. Scenario results show what would need to happen, not what a redesign will automatically deliver. Keep non-financial goals, such as easier management or a clearer customer experience, visible alongside the numbers.

The contribution-based approach follows the reasoning behind the [U.S. Small Business Administration's break-even guidance](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/calculate-your-startup-costs/break-even-point). The website-specific scenarios above are an adaptation for this guide, rather than an SBA forecast or prescribed website ROI model.
